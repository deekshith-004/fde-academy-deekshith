Exercise 1 — Facilitate a 60-Minute Mock Discovery Workshop

Task 1 — Prepare Your Facilitation Plan

Framing Statement

“The purpose of today's workshop is to understand how shipment exceptions and delays are handled today, identify the biggest pain points, and agree on what an improved future process should look like. By the end, we should have a clear current-state process, a desired future state, and a prioritized list of problems to address.”

Opening Current-State Question

“Could you walk me through what happens today when a shipment is delayed, starting from the moment the delay is noticed until the customer is updated?”

Task 2 — Run the Workshop

Phase 2 — Current State (As-Is)

A shipment becomes delayed or its status needs to be checked.

The dispatcher checks the TMS for transit status.

The dispatcher checks the warehouse system for physical location.

The dispatcher searches email threads for warehouse notes.

The dispatcher combines information from the three systems.

A summary is manually prepared and the customer is contacted.

If more information is needed, the dispatcher contacts the warehouse and waits for a response.

Phase 3 — Pain Points

Ops Director: The team often learns about delays only after the customer calls to complain.

Dispatcher: Each status inquiry requires checking three systems, taking around 10–15 minutes.

Dispatcher: Information is spread across the TMS, warehouse system, and email threads.

IT Lead: The three systems are not properly integrated because a legacy integration was never completed.

Ops Director: There is no consistent real-time visibility into delayed shipments.

Dispatcher: There is limited traceability of shipment and rerouting decisions.

Phase 4 — Future State (To-Be)

A unified, real-time shipment visibility process should combine information from the TMS, warehouse system, and relevant sources in one place. Delay risks should be identified proactively, the right operations users should receive alerts, and dispatchers should be able to act without manually checking multiple systems. Important decisions should have an audit trail.

Task 3 — As-Is / To-Be Map

AS-IS: Manual Shipment Status and Exception Handling

Shipment/status inquiry → Dispatcher checks TMS → Checks warehouse system → Checks email → Combines information manually → Contacts warehouse if needed → Updates customer

Pain points: Three-system lookup, 10–15 minute inquiries, no shared visibility, reactive delay discovery, integration gap, limited audit trail.

TO-BE: Unified and Proactive Shipment Exception Management

Shipment data integrated → Unified shipment view → Delay risk identified → Relevant user alerted → Dispatcher takes action → Customer notified proactively → Decision recorded

Reflection Questions

1. Did the IT Lead's legacy integration detail come up naturally?

The technical detail did not come up immediately because the IT Lead revealed it only after being asked about how the systems exchange information. This shows why an FDE should actively hear from technical stakeholders as well as business users.

2. How did you handle the Ops Director's assumption?

I treated it as an assumption rather than a confirmed fact and asked for a specific example. This acknowledged the concern without directly blaming the warehouse team and kept the discussion fact-based.

3. If only 4 of 6 phases were possible, which would you cut?

I would shorten rather than completely remove Framing and Close. If two phases had to be cut, I would reduce the detailed Future-State discussion and shorten the Close, while protecting Current State, Pain Points, and Prioritization.
